# Judging, submission, rules

Source: Junction 2026 Participant Guidebook (verbatim excerpts).

## Evaluation criteria
INNOVATION
- Is the idea/concept/prototype new or different compared to the already presented ones?
- Does the idea/concept/prototype or some parts of it distinguish somehow from the already presented ones?

IMPACT
- Does the idea/concept/prototype have a significant impact on society through solving the problem(s) it addresses?
- Does the idea/concept/prototype have a significant impact on the majority of the people facing the problem it solves - how large is the ratio between the number of people whose problem got solved compared to the total people affected by the problem?

IMPLEMENTATION
- Is the idea/concept/prototype eventually realistic, viable and feasible to be implemented from all perspectives?

After submitting the projects, partner and peer reviewing will start in parallel. All hackers need to be present at the venue for the live peer reviewing on Sunday (15th), 10:30–14:00.
To ensure quality results, each participant should review the projects assigned to them. In case the team pitching are not at their assigned table, you will have the opportunity to press the "not present" button. If you or your team do not take part in the peer reviewing, you are not eligible for the main prize.
Partners select the challenge winners and runner-ups.
The overall TOP 5 projects are determined based on the peer reviewing. TOP teams are instructed for the pitches in the closing ceremony for 3 minutes each and afterwards there will be 3 minutes for questions, then our panel of 7 judges vote on the best projects.

Every partner will determine judging criteria that will determines what they are looking for in a project. Remember to check carefully the judging criteria of the challenge your team is doing.

## Challenges
On Friday (13th November) at 23:59 o'clock you are asked to submit which challenge you are planning to tackle. You are allowed to change/submit a different challenge than the challenge you chose.
You are allowed to submit a project for multiple challenges.

## Project submission
The final submission deadline is on Sunday the 15th at 10:00.
The submission form closes automatically, and unfortunately we won't accept any late submissions.
Set your project to FINAL from DRAFT before the final deadline. Draft projects are not reviewed. You can edit the submissions at any time before the deadline, even the final projects.

For the final submission we require:
- Project name, punchline, description
- Challenge
- Technologies used
- 2 minute long video pitch of the final project, this can be uploaded to the preferred platform such as YouTube or Vimeo, but it has to be available for everyone to enjoy. And to be very frank: not a second longer than 2 minutes, and we will be very strict with this.
- Demo and source code links
- Source code publicity
- Do you want to be credited for the project?
- Pitch deck (pdf)
- Phone numbers of your team members (we will call you if you win something)
- Discord username for contacting purposes if you don't answer your phone

Platform steps:
4. One member from the team creates a project submission.
5. By Friday 23:59 submit a draft for a team count.
6. FINAL submission by Sunday 10:00.

## Pitching
- The top 5 teams will pitch on stage, chosen by peer reviewing
- Please practice your pitch beforehand. There will be no time to separately practice the pitch after the review period has ended.
- Each team has exactly 3 min for their pitch. After the time is up, the mics will be killed.
- After pitching, judges will have 3 minutes to ask questions from you.
- One hand held mic per team. We advise that one person is on stage.
- You will have a chance to demo your project on the stage from your own computer. We will check that you are able to connect your computer beforehand.

## Junction rules
To be eligible for prizes, including the 10 000 € main prize (total prize pool 100 000 €), you must follow these rules:
- No code written before the event.
- Allowed team size is 1 – 4.
- You must participate in at least one challenge.
- You and your team must take part in the live peer reviewing on Sunday to be eligible for the main prize.
- The project submission must contain a pitch video, maximum 2 minutes long.
- You must submit a link to your source. All code outside of open source libraries must be written at the event. If you use any code written specifically for this idea in advance, or work on a new project that is somehow related to an already existing company, you will be disqualified. All project submissions are licensed under the MIT open-source license, and organizers must be able to access your source code.

Partner APIs and other material used is subject to the partner terms.
